Business Operations Data

LA Global · AI Strategy & Advisory

Most AI conversations in media start with audience data or content archives. The data a business actually runs on — how work moves, what it costs, where it stalls — is usually the last asset anyone looks at, and often the fastest one to return something.

What it is

Business operations data is the record your company generates by running itself. It is distinct from the audience and content data that dominates most media AI discussions.

In a media or technology business it typically sits across ad operations and order management, traffic and scheduling, rights and contract systems, finance and billing, CRM and sales pipeline, production and post workflows, support ticketing, and HR and resourcing systems.

Individually these look like plumbing. Together they are a detailed account of how the business actually operates — which is precisely what an AI system needs in order to do anything useful about it.

Why it gets overlooked

Three reasons, in our experience.

It is nobody's asset. Audience data has an owner — a chief data officer, a revenue leader, someone whose number depends on it. Operations data is spread across finance, ad ops, and IT, each treating their slice as a system of record rather than an asset.

It is unglamorous. Board conversations gravitate to content and audience because that is where the brand lives. Order management exceptions do not present well, even when they are where the margin is.

It looks like a technology project. Framed as a data warehouse initiative, it competes for budget against things with clearer revenue stories and usually loses. Framed as an operating margin question, it competes differently.

Where the value sits

AreaWhat becomes possible
Operational efficiency Finding where cycle time is actually lost rather than where people assume it is. Process data exposes the handoffs, rework loops, and exception paths that consume capacity invisibly.
Agent deployment Agents need a defined process, clean inputs, and a record of what good looks like. Operations data is where all three come from. Without it, agent projects stall at the pilot stage because nothing can be evaluated.
Decision support Pricing, inventory, resourcing, and renewal decisions made against what the business has actually done, rather than against a quarterly roll-up that arrives too late to act on.
Deal and licensing readiness Rights, contract, and usage systems determine what you can license and on what terms. When that record is fragmented, data monetization conversations stall in diligence — not over price, but over provenance.

The fourth row is the one most often missed. Operations data is rarely the asset being licensed, but it is frequently what proves the asset is licensable.

What readiness looks like

Four questions tell you most of what you need to know:

  • Can you produce an inventory? A documented list of operational systems, what each holds, who owns it, and where it is authoritative.
  • Do the systems agree? Where the same entity — a client, a campaign, a title, an employee — exists in several systems, is it resolvable to one identity, or does reconciliation happen manually?
  • Is history intact? Many operational systems overwrite rather than append. If prior states are not retained, the data describes the present but cannot explain how you got here.
  • Is access governed? Operations data carries employee, client, and commercial sensitivity. Opening it up without controls creates exposure faster than it creates value.

Few organizations answer yes to all four at the outset. The answers determine sequence, not eligibility.

Where it goes wrong

Boiling the ocean. Unifying every operational system before doing anything is a multi-year programme that usually loses its sponsor before it finishes. Pick the process where the cost is clearest and prove it there.

Treating it as a reporting problem. A dashboard describes what happened. The value here is in systems that act — routing, flagging, drafting, reconciling — which demands a different standard of data quality than reporting ever did.

Underestimating the people dimension. Operations data is a record of how individuals work. Deploying it without being explicit about purpose, and without consulting the people it describes, turns an efficiency programme into a trust problem. Employment and works council obligations apply in several jurisdictions.

How we engage

We typically start with a scoped diagnostic across one or two operational domains rather than an enterprise-wide audit — establishing what exists, what it would take to make it usable, and which processes justify the work on their own numbers.

From there the work splits into a sequencing plan the board can fund in stages, and hands-on support through the first deployment, so the first use case proves the approach rather than consuming the budget.
